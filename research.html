Research Interests

What happens when a solution exists, but computation reaches its limit?

My research sits where analysis, computation, and partial differential equations meet. I am interested in what information is needed to determine the size of a solution — and what happens when that information is absent.

Central theme

Computability of PDEs

The starting point is a practical one: which assumptions on the initial data make the size of a solution computable?

When those assumptions are absent, familiar ideas can give way to uncertainty principles. My work examines where these boundaries lie and how they change across different classes of equations.

01

Hyperbolic PDEs

Adapting computability methods to equations that describe wave-like propagation.

02

Dispersive PDEs

Studying how computation interacts with solutions whose different frequencies travel at different speeds.

03

Parabolic PDEs

Extending the same questions to equations associated with diffusion and smoothing behaviour.

A second direction

Dynamic Mode Decomposition

I am also interested in variations of Dynamic Mode Decomposition for continuous-time dynamical systems.

In this setting, finite-difference approximations are typically used to recover the evolution of data. This creates another meeting point between continuous mathematics and the discrete tools used to understand it.

Questions behind the work

  • What information makes a solution computationally accessible?
  • What can still be known when that information is incomplete?
  • How can finite-difference approximations recover the evolution of data?
